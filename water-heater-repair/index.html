Emergency Water Heater Repair in Plainfield, NJ

A water heater that has stopped heating or started leaking is an emergency in a Plainfield winter, and emergency water heater repair is available 24/7. Shut off the cold water supply to the tank if it is leaking, leave the power or gas alone, and call (908) 357-1542.

When a water heater problem cannot wait

No hot water is uncomfortable. A leak is a different matter, because a tank can release dozens of gallons onto a basement or garage floor before anyone notices. We prioritize leaking tanks, tripped relief valves that keep running and any heater that smells of gas or smoke.

  • Water pooling around the base of the tank, or dripping from the relief valve discharge pipe.
  • A rotten-egg smell, a scorched smell or any gas odor near a gas-fired heater.
  • Rumbling, popping or banging that is getting louder.
  • Hot water that runs out in minutes, or no hot water at all after the pilot or breaker was checked.

Safe steps before the plumber arrives

  1. Shut off the cold water to the tank. The shutoff valve sits on the cold line above the heater. Turning it clockwise stops water entering the tank and slows a leak.
  2. Leave power alone if water is near it. For an electric heater, turn off the breaker only if you can reach the panel without standing in water. Otherwise wait for us.
  3. Gas smell means leave. If you smell gas, leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 first. Call us after you are safe.
  4. Move belongings off the floor. Lift boxes and stored items away from the tank so the water has less to damage.

Common failures we repair

Most repairs fall into a short list. On electric tanks, a failed lower heating element or thermostat accounts for many no-hot-water calls. On gas tanks, a dirty or failing thermocouple, a pilot that will not stay lit or a faulty gas control valve is the usual cause.

Tanks also fail from the inside. Sediment settles at the bottom and bakes into a hard layer that makes the burner work harder and produces a rumbling sound. The anode rod, a sacrificial metal rod that protects the tank lining, wears out over time. Once it is gone, the steel tank starts to corrode.

A leak from a fitting, a drain valve or the relief valve can often be repaired. A leak from the tank body cannot. That is the point where water heater replacement makes more sense than another repair visit, and we tell you straight when we reach that conclusion.

Maintenance advice from the City of Plainfield

The City's own homeowner manual from the Division of Inspections gives two simple habits. It says to drain sediment from the hot water tank every six months, or every three months if the water has high mineral content. It also says to trip the pressure relief valve once a year, and if no water comes out, the valve needs replacing.

Those habits work because sediment and a stuck relief valve are two of the quieter causes of heater trouble. If your tank has never been drained, ask us to flush it before it gets noisy. If the valve will not release when you lift the lever, call us rather than forcing it.

Age matters, and the manufacturer's date code on the data plate tells you how old the tank is. We read it on every visit and give you the comparison in plain numbers. If a tank is close to the end of its life, a new unit is often the better use of money than a third repair.

Hot water heating and no-heat calls

Many older Plainfield homes heat with hot water boilers, and some of those boilers also supply the domestic hot water. If your radiators are cold and your hot water is weak, the problem may sit in the boiler rather than a separate tank. Our boiler repair page covers what we check first.
