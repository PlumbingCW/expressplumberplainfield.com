Repiping in Plainfield, NJ

Repiping in Plainfield, NJ replaces worn supply lines before they fail, and the right plan depends on what is in your walls. Call (908) 357-1542 and we will look at your pipes and explain your options.

Signs your pipes may need replacing

Low water pressure

Corroded galvanized pipe narrows from the inside and chokes the flow.

Brown or rusty water

Rust-colored water that clears slowly can mean the pipes are corroding.

Repeated leaks

More than one pinhole leak on the same line means the pipe wall is failing.

Visible corrosion

Green crust on copper, rust on steel or flaking at fittings are warnings.

One leak does not mean you need to replace everything. Several leaks in a short time on the same material usually does. If you are chasing leaks, our leak detection page explains how we confirm where they are.

Whole-house or partial repipe

A whole-house repipe replaces all the supply lines with new pipe from the main shutoff to the fixtures. A partial repipe replaces one section, such as the line to a bathroom or the run between the basement and the second floor. A partial job is the right choice when the damage is local and the rest of the pipe is sound.

We explain which approach fits after we trace the lines, test the pressure and look at the pipe material behind the walls. Where the walls are open, as in a remodel, the cost of replacing more pipe at the same time is lower.

Plainfield note: galvanized pipe and lead service lines

Many Plainfield houses were built between the 1870s and the 1930s, and many were piped in galvanized steel when it was standard. Galvanized steel corrodes from the inside over decades, which causes low pressure and rusty water well before it leaks.

The pipe between the water main and your house is a separate piece of the system. Under New Jersey law, water systems must replace lead service lines within 10 years of the law's start, by July 2031, and galvanized service lines are presumed to include lead. The state's NJDEP lead service line replacement FAQ explains who pays and how it works. If your utility replaces the service line, the best time to replace old interior galvanized is around the same visit. Our water service line repair page covers the outside piece.

What a repipe involves

  1. Trace the existing lines and mark where walls or ceilings need access.
  2. Shut off the water, drain the system and cut in the new main run.
  3. Run new lines to each fixture, usually through basements, closets and existing chases.
  4. Connect fixtures, test the pressure and check every joint.
  5. Patch access openings and restore water.

We plan the work to keep the house usable. Most whole-house repipes can be done in a few days, depending on size and access, and we explain where water will be off.

What affects the price of repiping

  • Number of fixtures and stories.More fixtures and more floors mean more pipe and more access.
  • Pipe material.PEX and copper differ in material and labor cost.
  • Wall and ceiling access.Finished plaster and tile cost more to open and restore.
  • Permits and inspection.Repiping typically needs a permit and an inspection.
  • Related repairs.Old valves, a new pressure regulator or a new main shutoff are common additions.
