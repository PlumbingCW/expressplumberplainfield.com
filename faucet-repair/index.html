When a faucet or sink problem is an emergency

A drip is a slow waste. A split supply line, a faucet that sprays or a shutoff valve that will not close is an emergency, because water keeps running under pressure. We book same-day visits for urgent sink calls when a technician is free.

  • Water spraying or pouring from a faucet body, handle or supply line.
  • A shutoff valve that will not turn or will not stop the flow.
  • A cabinet floor that is wet or swelling under the sink.
  • A sink that fills and will not drain while the faucet cannot be turned off.

What to do while you wait

  1. Close the angle stops. The two small valves under the sink control hot and cold. Turn each clockwise. If they will not move, do not force them.
  2. Shut the main if needed. If water keeps coming, close the main water valve where the line enters the house.
  3. Empty the cabinet. Remove cleaning products and set a bucket under the leak.
  4. Turn off nearby power. If water is near outlets or a disposal, cut power at the breaker from a dry spot.

Why faucets leak

Most drips come from a worn cartridge, washer or O-ring inside the faucet. Handles that leak at the base usually have a failed seal. A spray hose that leaks under the sink may have a split line or a loose connection. Because each faucet type fails in a different way, we identify the brand and model before ordering parts.

Repair or replace the faucet

A repair is the better choice when the faucet is a quality model with a failed internal part. Replacement is better when the body is corroded, parts are no longer made or the finish has failed. For an older fixture, the cost of repair parts sometimes approaches the price of a new faucet.

We also replace shutoff valves and supply lines during the visit when they are old or stiff. A new angle stop costs little and means the next repair starts with a valve that works.

Plainfield note: old valves and mixed pipe

In many older Plainfield houses, the valves under the sinks have not been turned in decades. They can seize or leak when finally operated. Behind the wall, the supply may be copper, galvanized steel or both, joined at different times. We test the valve gently, and if it will not hold, we shut off at the main and replace it.

If the supply piping itself is old and corroded, see repiping for when replacing more than the fixture makes sense.

Sink drains and disposals

Sink problems are often drain problems. A slow kitchen sink with a good faucet usually has a grease clog, and our drain cleaning page explains the fix. A sink with a disposal that hums or leaks may need garbage disposal repair.
