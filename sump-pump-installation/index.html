Emergency Sump Pump Repair and Installation in Plainfield, NJ

A sump pump that fails during a storm can flood a Plainfield, NJ basement within hours, and emergency sump pump repair is available 24/7. Move valuables up, stay out of standing water near electrical outlets, and call (908) 357-1542.

When a sump pump problem is an emergency

A sump pump matters most on the worst day, so a failure during heavy rain is an emergency. Water in a basement can reach outlets, a furnace and a water heater quickly, and we book same-day visits when we have a technician free.

  • Water rising in the pit with a pump that does not start.
  • A pump that runs constantly and cannot keep up.
  • Standing water in the basement with a power outage in the area.
  • A pump that has hummed, rattled or tripped the breaker in recent storms.

What to do while you wait

  1. Stay out of water near electricity. If water is near outlets, a panel, a furnace or a water heater, do not step into it. Call an electrician or the utility if you must cut power.
  2. Check the pump's power. Make sure it is plugged in, the GFCI has not tripped and the breaker is on.
  3. Check the float. A float that is stuck against the pit wall will not start the pump. Lift it gently if you can reach safely.
  4. Move valuables up. Lift boxes, electronics and furniture off the floor.
  5. Call us. Tell us whether the pump hums, runs or is silent.

Plainfield note: floods, Cedar Brook and storm backups

Plainfield has a long history with floodwater. The Cedar Brook channel through the city was designed to catch and drain floodwater, and the Robinson's Branch of the Rahway River also runs through town. When the ground is saturated, basements in low areas take on water even without a river overflow.

In September 2021, the remnants of Hurricane Ida brought flash flooding to the area. North Plainfield's own press release described homes damaged, vehicles swept away and a list kept by the fire department of residents needing basement pump-outs. A working pump and a backup are worth having before the next storm, and not after it.

Backup power and a second pump

Storms that flood basements often knock out electricity at the same time. A battery backup system keeps a second pump running when the main one has no power, and a water-powered backup is an option where code allows. We size the battery to the inflow you actually see.

Battery backup pump

A second pump on a battery and charger that starts when the main pump or power fails.

Alarm

A high-water alarm warns you before the basement floods.

Check valve

A one-way valve stops the pumped water from flowing back into the pit.

Proper discharge

A discharge line that runs far enough from the foundation and does not freeze.

Test the pump twice a year by pouring water into the pit until the float lifts. If it does not start, call us. For a wider basement water plan, see basement waterproofing.

Sump pump installation

A new installation starts with the pit. If the basement has a pit, we replace the pump, the float and the check valve. If it does not, we may cut the floor to install a pit and a drain loop to feed it. We run a discharge line outside to a spot that drains away from the house and does not freeze.

We check local rules on where the water can go. Discharging into the sanitary sewer is generally not allowed for sump water, and we plan the line accordingly. Ask the Construction Division at 908-226-2665 if you are unsure.

Code notes for sump pump work

Sump pump plumbing falls under the state Plumbing Subcode, N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15, and the electrical side falls under the Electrical Subcode. A pump should sit on its own protected circuit, and a pump cord should never be run through a wet floor.

Where the discharge may go differs by municipality. The Plainfield Construction Division can tell you what is allowed on your lot.
