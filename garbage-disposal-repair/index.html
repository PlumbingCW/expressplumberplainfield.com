When a disposal problem is an emergency

A disposal that hums and will not turn is a repair call, not a panic. One that leaks into the cabinet, backs up the kitchen sink or sparks is urgent. We book same-day visits for urgent disposal calls when a technician is free.

  • Water pooling under the sink from the disposal body or its drain connections.
  • A sink that fills with dirty water and will not drain.
  • A burning smell, smoke or sparks from the unit.
  • A disposal that trips the breaker whenever it is switched on.

Safe steps before we arrive

  1. Turn off the switch and the breaker. Never put your hand into a disposal, even when it seems off. Cut power at the breaker if you can reach it from a dry spot.
  2. Stop running water into the sink. A blocked disposal will not take more water.
  3. Use the hex-key hole if you know how. Many disposals have a hex socket on the underside. Turning it by hand with the power off can free a jam. Do not use force.
  4. Press the reset button. A small red button on the bottom resets an overloaded motor. Wait a few minutes first.

Disposals do not last forever. A unit that leaks from the housing cannot be repaired, and one with a seized motor that keeps tripping the breaker is usually at the end of its life.

What not to put down a disposal

  • Grease, oil and fat, which cool and coat the pipes.
  • Coffee grounds and eggshells in large amounts, which pack into sludge.
  • Fibrous foods like celery, corn husks and artichokes.
  • Pasta, rice and potato peels, which swell and clog.
  • Bones, fruit pits and non-food items.

A disposal is not a trash can. Treat it as a way to grind small scraps and run plenty of cold water while it works.

Disposal installation and replacement

A new disposal fits into the existing sink flange if the mounting is compatible. We remove the old unit, install the new mount, connect the drain and the dishwasher inlet if you have one, and wire the unit to a switch or a plug. We also test for leaks and run it under load.

Choose horsepower by use. A small household is fine with a lower-horsepower unit, and a family that cooks a lot benefits from a stronger one with a better grinding system. We recommend a unit after a quick talk about your kitchen. If the sink also drains slowly, see drain cleaning because the cause may be the line and not the unit.

Plainfield note: older kitchens and drain lines

Many Plainfield kitchens were built before disposals were common, and the sink drain may connect to an older cast iron or galvanized stack. Grease from the disposal settles in those older pipes and builds up. If a kitchen sink backs up repeatedly even with the disposal working, the line behind it may need cleaning or a camera look.

Dishwasher and disposal connections often share the same drain. A clog there can send dirty water back into the dishwasher, so check both when the sink backs up.
