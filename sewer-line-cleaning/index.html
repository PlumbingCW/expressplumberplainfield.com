Sewer backup: what to do first

A main line clog does not clear itself. Every fixture in the house drains into the same pipe, so when it stops, the lowest opening becomes the overflow. We treat active backups as urgent and book same-day visits when a crew is free.

  • Sewage or dirty water in the basement floor drain, tub or shower.
  • Toilets that gurgle or bubble when you run the washing machine.
  • Several fixtures draining slowly at once.
  • A sewage smell near the cleanout or in the yard.

While you wait

  1. Stop using every drain. No flushing, laundry or showers. The water has nowhere to go.
  2. Keep children and pets away. Treat standing water from a sewer line as contaminated.
  3. Find the cleanout. It is usually a capped pipe near the foundation, in the basement or in a crawl space. Do not open it if water is under pressure.
  4. Photograph the damage. Take dated photos before cleanup for your insurance claim.

How we clear a main line

We set up at the cleanout and send a cable machine with a cutting head through the blockage. The head cuts through paper, grease and soft roots and pulls debris back to the access point. After the line is flowing, we flush it and test the drains.

When the cable reveals heavy buildup or roots that keep returning, we recommend hydro jetting to clean the full pipe wall. If the cable catches or the line will not stay clear, we scope it with a sewer camera to find the cause.

Plainfield note: who maintains what

Wastewater from the city flows to the Plainfield Area Regional Sewerage Authority, which serves eight municipalities including Plainfield, North Plainfield and Scotch Plains. The pipe from your house to the main is generally the homeowner's responsibility to maintain. Ask your municipality where ownership changes at your address, because it affects who pays when the problem is under the street.

Heavy storms add a second risk. After Hurricane Ida's remnants flooded the area in September 2021, North Plainfield's own press release described widespread flooding and emergency response. Lines that flow slowly in dry weather can back up fast when the ground is saturated, so a clean lateral is worth having before the rainy season.

Preventing the next backup

  • Flush only toilet paper. Wipes labeled flushable do not break down in a lateral.
  • Keep grease out of the sink. Wipe pans before washing and bin the fat.
  • Know where your cleanout is and keep it accessible.
  • Clear a root-prone line on a schedule, not only after a backup.
  • Scope an old lateral once so you know its real condition.

If the pipe turns out to be damaged, we explain it plainly and point you to sewer line repair for the options.
