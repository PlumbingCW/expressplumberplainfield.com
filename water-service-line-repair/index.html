Emergency Water Service Line Repair in Plainfield, NJ

When a service line problem is an emergency

The service line is the buried pipe that brings water from the street main to your house. When it breaks, the water can pour into the ground or the basement, and the supply drops. We book same-day visits when we have a crew free.

  • Water gushing in the yard or bubbling through pavement.
  • A sudden drop to no pressure or no water in the whole house.
  • A wet basement wall where the line enters the house.
  • A water bill that jumped with no leak inside.

What to do while you wait

  1. Close the main valve inside. It is usually near the meter. If it will not close, call the utility.
  2. Check the meter. With everything off, a moving meter means water is leaving the system.
  3. Call the utility if the leak is on their side. New Jersey American Water maintains the main and the meter connection. We help you work out where responsibility changes.
  4. Keep people away from the wet area. Saturated soil near a trench or foundation can shift.

Who owns which part of the line

The utility generally owns the main and the portion up to the curb or meter. The homeowner owns the rest to the house. The boundary depends on the utility's rules and your address, so ask New Jersey American Water where it sits for your property before assuming either side.

We find the leak, identify which side it is on and tell you plainly who should handle it. If it is yours, we give you a repair price. If it is theirs, we tell you who to call.

Plainfield note: lead and galvanized service lines

New Jersey law requires water systems to replace lead service lines within 10 years of the law's start, by July 2031, and treats galvanized service lines as presumed to contain lead. The state's NJDEP lead service line replacement FAQ explains the process, who pays for what and what homeowners should do.

If you hire a contractor to replace your side of the line, the state FAQ says the contractor should coordinate with the water system for the shutoff. We handle that coordination. The water system serving Plainfield is New Jersey American Water, and its service territory information is published on its water quality page.

What affects the price of service line repair

  • Length and depth.A short shallow line costs less than a long deep one.
  • Pipe material.Lead, galvanized, copper and plastic each need different handling.
  • Surface restoration.Pavement, sidewalk and landscaping repairs add cost.
  • Utility coordination.Shutoffs and inspections affect scheduling.
  • Permits.Excavation and plumbing work may require them.
