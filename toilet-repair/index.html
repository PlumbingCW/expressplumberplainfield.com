When a toilet problem is an emergency

A running toilet is a nuisance. An overflowing one is an emergency, because water and waste spread across a bathroom floor and into the ceiling below. We book same-day visits for urgent toilet calls when we have a technician free.

  • A toilet that overflows when flushed or will not stop filling.
  • Water leaking at the base, the tank or the supply line.
  • A toilet backing up with other fixtures also slow or gurgling.
  • The only toilet in the house is out of service.

What to do before we arrive

  1. Close the shutoff valve. Turn the valve on the wall or floor behind the toilet clockwise until it stops. This ends the fill.
  2. Lift the tank lid if it keeps running. Raise the float or lift the flapper chain to stop the flow while you wait.
  3. Do not keep flushing. More flushes add more water to a blocked drain.
  4. Mop up and ventilate. Clean standing water quickly and keep children and pets away.

If more than one fixture is slow, the problem is in the drain line rather than the toilet. Our drain cleaning page explains how we tell the difference.

Toilet installation and replacement

A toilet that has cracked, rocks no matter how many times it is reset, or wastes water on every flush is a candidate for replacement. We remove the old one, inspect the flange and the floor, set the new toilet on a new wax ring or seal and test for leaks.

Choose a model that fits the rough-in distance, the distance from the wall to the center of the drain. Most are 12 inches, but older homes vary. We measure before you buy, so the toilet you pick will fit. We also check the shutoff valve and the supply line, which are cheap to replace while the toilet is off.

Plainfield note: older bathrooms

Many Plainfield houses date from the late 1800s and early 1900s, and their bathrooms have been remodeled in pieces. A new toilet may sit on a floor flange that was set for an older fixture, or on a subfloor that has softened from years of small leaks. We check the floor and the flange before closing it up, because a rotted subfloor makes any toilet rock.

If a slow leak has been reaching the ceiling below, our leak detection page explains how we find the source.

What affects the price of toilet repair

  • Repair or replacement.A flapper or fill valve costs far less than a new toilet.
  • Floor and flange condition.Rot or a damaged flange adds repair work.
  • Fixture choice.Standard, comfort-height and wall-hung toilets vary widely in cost.
  • Shutoff and supply line.Old valves that will not close are replaced.
  • Time of day.After-hours dispatch is priced differently from a scheduled visit.
