Emergency Hydro Jetting in Plainfield, NJ

A sewer or main drain backup in Plainfield, NJ is an emergency, and emergency hydro jetting is available 24/7. Stop using water, keep everyone out of the affected area, and call (908) 357-1542 to book a technician.

When a backup is an emergency

Jetting is often the follow-up to a backup that a cable could not fully fix. If the line has backed up and is full of grease or scale, jetting clears the pipe wall instead of poking a hole through the middle of the blockage. We treat active backups as urgent and book same-day visits when we have capacity.

  • Sewage or gray water rising in a basement drain, tub or toilet.
  • A main line that has been cleared more than once this year and is blocked again.
  • A restaurant or multi-unit building line that backs up repeatedly.
  • Slow drains in every fixture with a gurgle when water runs elsewhere.

What to do before the jetting crew arrives

  1. Stop sending water down the line. Hold laundry, dishwashers and showers. Extra water has nowhere to go.
  2. Locate and clear the cleanout. A cleanout fitting gives the jetting hose direct access. Clear stored items from around it.
  3. Protect the floor. Cover or remove rugs near the access point. Jetting can bring up water and debris.
  4. Tell us what has been tried. Mention prior snaking, chemical cleaners or camera visits. It changes how we approach the line.

How hydro jetting works

A jetting machine pumps water at high pressure through a hose with a specialized nozzle. Jets point backward to pull the hose forward and forward to cut at the blockage. The spray scours the entire pipe wall, removes grease and scale, and flushes the debris downstream.

A cable machine bores a hole through a clog. A jetter cleans the pipe. That difference is why lines that keep clogging often stay clear for much longer after a proper jetting pass.

When we do not jet

Jetting is not right for every pipe. A cracked, collapsed or severely offset line can be made worse by high-pressure water, and a belly full of standing water needs to be cleared first. For that reason we run a camera before a heavy jetting pass, and we explain what we saw. If a pipe is not sound, the right next step is sewer line repair, not more cleaning.

A sewer camera inspection before and after jetting also gives you a record of the pipe's condition, which helps with landlord, insurance and resale conversations.

Plainfield note: older laterals and mature trees

Plainfield's older neighborhoods have lots of mature street trees, and many of their sewer laterals are decades old. Clay joints and cast iron both give roots and scale a place to start. That is why jetting is a common choice here for lines that clog every spring when roots grow.

Wastewater from the city flows to the Plainfield Area Regional Sewerage Authority, which serves eight municipalities. Keeping your lateral clear helps you, and it also reduces the chance of a blockage in the shared system downstream.
