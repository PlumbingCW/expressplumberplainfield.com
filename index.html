What to do in a plumbing emergency

The first few minutes decide how much damage you get. Most emergencies come down to stopping the water, making the area safe and calling someone who can come. Here is what to do while you wait for our plumber.

  • Burst or leaking pipe: close the main water valve, usually near the meter where the line enters the basement.
  • Sewage or drain backup: stop using every drain and keep people away from standing water.
  • Water heater leak: close the cold supply valve on top of the tank and switch off its gas or power.
  • Gas smell: leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 first, then call us.

Why Plainfield plumbing needs local knowledge

Plainfield grew from about 5,100 residents in 1870 to more than 34,000 by 1930, and a great deal of that housing is still here. The Crescent Area Historic District alone holds 111 contributing buildings, most built between 1870 and 1900. Houses of that age have been re-piped in pieces, and a plumber who has seen cast iron drains, clay laterals and galvanized supply lines knows what to expect before opening a wall.

That is why we start with a diagnosis instead of a guess. A slow drain in a house like that may be grease, a root or a collapsed section. A weak shower may be a clogged aerator or a supply pipe that has narrowed from the inside. We tell you which, and what it will take to fix.

Water, brooks and storms in Plainfield

Plainfield sits between Cedar Brook and the Robinson's Branch of the Rahway River, and the Cedar Brook channel was built to catch and drain the city's floodwater. When Hurricane Ida's remnants hit in September 2021, neighboring North Plainfield reported damaged homes and a fire department list of residents needing basement pump-outs. Basements here are worth protecting with a working sump pump and a clear drain.

Water service comes from New Jersey American Water, and wastewater flows to the Plainfield Area Regional Sewerage Authority, which serves eight municipalities. The pipe from your house to the main is generally the homeowner's responsibility. If you are not sure where ownership changes at your address, ask the city before you plan any work.

What affects the price of a plumbing job

We do not quote a number before we know what is wrong, because a number without a diagnosis is a guess. What we do is explain the price before any work begins, so you can say yes or no with the facts in front of you.

  • What failed.A worn washer and a collapsed sewer line are very different jobs.
  • Access.An open basement is quicker to work in than a finished wall or a tight crawl space.
  • Parts and materials.Brand, pipe type and fixture quality change the total.
  • Permits.Some jobs, such as a new water heater, need a permit under the City's homeowner manual.
  • Time of day.After-hours dispatch is priced differently from a scheduled weekday visit.

Many homeowner policies cover sudden water damage but not sewer backup unless you added that coverage. Read yours before you need it. Photograph any damage before cleanup.

Permits and licensed work in Plainfield

The City of Plainfield's homeowner manual says installing a hot water heater, rearranging plumbing pipes and installing new bathroom fixtures with rearranged piping all need a permit, while ordinary maintenance does not. The Construction Division at 908-226-2665 can tell you what applies to your project.

New Jersey also licenses master plumbers through the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers, and the Board offers a license lookup. Check any plumber you hire, including us, before work starts.
