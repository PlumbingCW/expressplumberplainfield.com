About Plainfield Express Plumbing

Based on East Front Street in Plainfield, NJ and dispatching 24/7 to the towns around it.

Call (908) 357-1542

Who we are

Plainfield Express Plumbing is a plumbing service based at 168 East Front Street in Plainfield, New Jersey. We handle emergency and planned plumbing work for homes and businesses in Plainfield, North Plainfield, Scotch Plains, South Plainfield, Piscataway and Fanwood. Our dispatch line is open 24/7, so a burst pipe at midnight reaches a person instead of a voicemail.

We do not claim years in business, awards or customer counts on this site, because we would rather you judge us on what we explain and what we do. What we can promise is how we work.

How we work

  1. We diagnose firstA plumber finds the cause before recommending a repair, so you pay to fix the problem and not a guess.
  2. We explain the price before work startsYou get the repair and the price in plain terms, and you decide.
  3. We say when a small fix is enoughIf a washer or a cartridge solves it, that is the repair we recommend.
  4. We tell you when the problem is biggerIf a pipe is failing or a tank is done, we show you why and what the options are.

Why local details matter

Plainfield's older neighborhoods, including the Crescent Area, Van Wyck Brooks and Netherwood Heights, have houses that are more than a century old. Their plumbing was added to and patched across generations. The city also has a flood history, with Cedar Brook running through it and Hurricane Ida's remnants flooding the area in September 2021. We write about those details on our service and area pages because they change how a job should be done. See our plumbing services and service areas.

How to verify any plumber

New Jersey licenses master plumbers through the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers, part of the Division of Consumer Affairs. The Board offers a license lookup, and we encourage you to check anyone you hire. For permits, the Plainfield Construction Division at 908-226-2665 can tell you what a project needs. The City's homeowner manual lists installing a hot water heater and rearranging plumbing pipes as permit work.

Questions about the work or the price are welcome. Call (908) 357-1542 at any hour.

What we do and do not claim

A plumbing company's website can say anything. We choose to say only what we can stand behind. We do not list years in business, because the number would not change whether your pipe is fixed correctly. We do not post reviews we cannot show you. We do not name staff we cannot introduce. We tell you our address, our phone number, our hours and our method.

Instead, the pages on this site show the thinking that goes into a job. They explain what causes a problem, what the options are and what each option costs you in time and disruption. If we get a fact wrong, tell us.

Emergency response, explained honestly

Our dispatch line is open 24/7. That means a person answers, asks what is happening and tells you what to do while a plumber is arranged. It does not mean a plumber is already parked outside your house. We book same-day visits when a plumber is available, and we tell you plainly if the wait will be long.

We never promise an arrival time we cannot control. Traffic, weather and other emergencies change the schedule. What we do promise is that you will hear what to expect when you call, and that you will know the price before work begins.

Residential, commercial and landlord work

Homeowners are most of our calls, from dripping faucets to sewer backups. Businesses call us for floor drains, water heaters and restroom fixtures. Landlords and property managers call for repeat clogs and tenant emergencies, and they get notes on which line, which unit and what we found.

Whatever the building, the method is the same. Diagnose first, explain next, repair after you agree. See our commercial plumbing page for businesses and our services index for the full list.

What you can expect when you call

The dispatcher will ask for your address, a short description of the problem and whether anyone is in danger. Expect questions about the main valve, the water heater and any gas smell, because those answers decide how fast we move. You can ask about the price structure, the permit and what the plumber will check first. No question is too basic.

If your problem is not an emergency, say so. We would rather schedule a planned visit at a time that suits you than send someone on a rush call that did not need one.
