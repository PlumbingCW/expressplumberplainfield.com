Emergency Water Heater Replacement in Plainfield, NJ

A water heater that has failed leaves a Plainfield, NJ household without hot water, and emergency water heater replacement is available 24/7. Shut off the cold water to the tank, switch off its gas or power, and call (908) 357-1542.

When a failed water heater is an emergency

Replacement is the right answer when the tank itself has failed, because a corroded steel shell cannot be patched. A tank that leaks from the body can release a lot of water quickly, so we treat it as urgent and book same-day visits when we have a technician free.

  • Water seeping from the tank body or pooling under it, not just from a pipe fitting.
  • Rusty or brown hot water that clears only after the tap runs for a while.
  • A tank that has stopped heating and shows rust at the base or on the jacket.
  • A heater over its expected life that has needed repeated repairs.

What to do while you wait

  1. Close the cold supply valve. Turn the handle on the cold line above the tank clockwise. This stops the tank refilling and slows the leak.
  2. Switch off the fuel or power. Turn the gas control to pilot or off, or flip the heater's breaker if you can reach the panel without stepping in water.
  3. Open one hot tap upstairs. This lets air into the line so the tank drains more easily when we connect a hose.
  4. Clear the floor. Move stored items away so the area is ready for the old tank to come out.

Is replacement really necessary?

Not every bad heater needs replacing. A failed element or thermostat is an inexpensive repair on a sound tank. Replacement makes sense when the tank has leaked from the shell, when it is near the end of its life, or when repair costs are approaching the price of a new unit.

We start by reading the data plate for the age and checking for rust and weeping. If a repair will buy you years, we say so and fix it. Our water heater repair page lists the faults that are worth repairing.

Size matters more than most people think. A tank that is too small runs out during a morning rush, and one that is too large costs more to heat. We size by the number of people, the bathrooms and the fixtures that run at the same time. If you are leaning toward a wall-hung unit, see our tankless water heater page for the trade-offs.

Permits, code and the old Plainfield basement

The City of Plainfield's own homeowner manual lists installing a hot water heater among the jobs that need a permit. Call the Construction Division at 908-226-2665 to confirm what your job needs. New Jersey requires plumbing work to be done by a licensed master plumber, and you can check any plumber on the state Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers license lookup.

Older Plainfield basements often hold a heater on a floor with a drain that is far from the unit, an old chimney flue that was sized for a boiler, or galvanized piping that has narrowed. We look at each of these before quoting, because they decide the real scope. A replacement that ignores the flue or the supply piping can leave you with a new tank that performs like the old one.

What a replacement visit includes

We drain and disconnect the old tank, set the new unit level, connect new flexible lines and a code-compliant relief valve discharge, and check the gas or electrical connection. Then we purge the air, set the temperature and walk you through the shutoff valve, the relief valve and the drain.

We also check for an expansion tank. Closed plumbing systems trap the extra volume as water heats, and without an expansion tank the pressure spikes can shorten the life of the new tank and its fittings. If your home has a pressure-reducing valve or a backflow device on the line, you probably need one.

What affects the price of a replacement

  • Tank size and type.A 40-gallon standard unit costs far less than a tankless system with venting.
  • Fuel and venting.Gas needs a safe flue. Electric may need a new circuit.
  • Code upgrades.Expansion tank, relief valve piping, seismic or drain pan requirements can add items.
  • Access and haul-away.Stairs, tight closets and old-tank disposal affect labor time.
  • Time of day.After-hours dispatch is priced differently from a scheduled weekday visit.
