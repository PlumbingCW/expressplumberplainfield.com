Plan the layout before the first cut

The most expensive mistakes in remodeling plumbing are moves that were not planned. Drain lines need slope, and vents need a path. A toilet or a tub can only move so far from the stack before the pipes need a new route. We look at where the existing lines run and show you which layouts are simple and which are costly.

Bring the fixture models if you have them. Rough-in dimensions and valve requirements differ by brand, and the right valve has to be in the wall before the tile goes up.

Basement bathrooms and below-grade work

A basement bathroom often sits below the sewer line, so the drain cannot run by gravity. In that case a sewage ejector pump collects waste and lifts it to the main drain. The system needs a sealed basin, a vent and a check valve.

If your house is prone to groundwater or storm backups, plan the basement fixtures with that in mind. A backwater valve, a sump pump and a battery backup are worth discussing. See sump pump installation and repair and basement waterproofing for those.

Plainfield note: permits and licensed work

The Plainfield Construction Division, reachable at 908-226-2665, enforces the New Jersey Uniform Construction Code and schedules inspections. Most new plumbing work needs a permit, and the Division can tell you exactly what your project requires. You can confirm a plumber's license through the state Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers license lookup.

Ask before you start, because unpermitted work can cause trouble at resale or with insurance.

How an installation visit goes

  1. Walk-through and measure. We look at the space, the existing lines and the fixtures you chose.
  2. Written price. You get a clear price covering materials, labor and permit items.
  3. Rough-in. We set supply and drain lines and the valves before walls close.
  4. Inspection. Where a permit applies, the inspector checks the rough-in.
  5. Finish and test. We set fixtures, test every connection and clean up.

Which code applies to new plumbing

New installations in New Jersey are built to the Plumbing Subcode, N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15, which adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code. It sets rules for venting, trap arms, fixture drains and supply sizing, and the inspector checks the work against it.

Fixtures worth planning around

Some fixtures need more rough-in planning than others. A freestanding tub needs a floor drain location and a floor-mounted or wall-mounted filler. A walk-in shower needs a drain with the right slope and a waterproof membrane. A pot filler over a stove needs a cold line in the wall. Decide these early, because moving a pipe after the wall is closed costs far more than placing it right the first time.
