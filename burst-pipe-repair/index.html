When a burst pipe is an emergency

Every burst pipe is urgent because water keeps flowing until the supply is closed. The main valve is the most important thing to find and turn. After that, we book same-day visits to repair the line when we have a technician free.

  • Water pouring from a ceiling, wall or floor.
  • A sudden loss of pressure with a hissing sound in the walls.
  • A frozen pipe that has split, often found when it thaws.
  • Water stains spreading quickly with no storm to explain them.

What to do in the first five minutes

  1. Shut off the main water valve. Turn it clockwise. In most Plainfield houses it is near the meter where the line enters the basement.
  2. Turn off power to wet areas. Cut the breaker from a dry spot if water is near outlets, fixtures or the panel.
  3. Open the lowest faucet. This drains the pipes and relieves pressure.
  4. Move belongings and soak up water. Lift items off the floor and start removing water to limit damage.
  5. Take photos. Document damage before cleanup for your insurance claim.

Why pipes burst

Winter freezing is the headline cause. Water expands as it freezes, and the pressure between the ice and a closed faucet splits the pipe. The split often sits where the ice formed, but the leak only shows up when it thaws.

Corrosion, age and pressure also cause bursts. A pipe with a thin wall can fail suddenly, and a pressure regulator that has failed can send high pressure into the whole system. Pinhole leaks from old pipe can open into larger splits.

Prevention is simple. Insulate pipes along exterior walls, shut off and drain outdoor hose bibs, keep the heat on while away and let a faucet drip on the coldest nights.

How we repair a burst pipe

We confirm the water is off and locate the split. We cut out the damaged section and replace it with new pipe and fittings that match the existing material. Then we pressure test the line and check nearby sections, since pipes that froze together often fail in more than one place.

If we find several leaks on the same old line, we explain the choice between repairing again and a larger job. See repiping for when that makes sense. For hidden leaks that follow a burst, leak detection confirms nothing else is wet.

Plainfield note: older houses and winter cold

Houses in Plainfield's older districts often have supply pipes in exterior walls, unheated porches and uninsulated crawl spaces where freezing is more likely. Additions and conversions added over the decades can leave a pipe in a cold pocket that the original builders did not have.

The water in these lines comes from New Jersey American Water, the utility serving Plainfield. If the break is on the service line between the street and the house, see water service line repair.
