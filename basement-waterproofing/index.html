Where basement water comes from

Water enters a basement for a few reasons. Groundwater pushes up through the floor or the joint where the floor meets the wall. Surface water runs toward the foundation because of grading or a clogged gutter. Foundation walls may be cracked or porous. A plumbing leak or a backed-up drain can also look like groundwater.

The first job is to sort those apart. We test the plumbing and the drains first, since a leaking pipe is a faster fix than a drainage system. Our leak detection page explains that test.

Plainfield note: flood history and older foundations

Plainfield sits along Cedar Brook and the Robinson's Branch of the Rahway River, and the Cedar Brook channel was built to catch and drain the city's floodwater. Even blocks away from the water, saturated ground can push moisture through older foundations.

Many houses here have fieldstone, brick or old block foundations laid more than a century ago. Mortar joints soften with age and let water through. Remedies for those walls differ from poured concrete, and we explain which applies to yours.

After Hurricane Ida in September 2021, North Plainfield reported homes damaged and a fire department list of residents needing basement pump-outs. Storms like that test any basement, and a plan made on a dry day works better than one made in a storm.

What we look at on a visit

  • Where water has marked the walls and floor, including efflorescence, the white mineral crust left behind.
  • The gutters, downspouts and the grading around the house.
  • The floor drain, sump pit and any existing pump.
  • Cracks, their width and whether they are moving.
  • The plumbing near the wet area.

We give you an honest read on whether water control is needed or a simpler fix will do. Sometimes a downspout extension solves what looked like a major problem.

Basement waterproofing and living space

If you plan to finish a basement, plan the water control first. Drywall and flooring over a wet wall invite mold. Check your sump system, keep the floor drain clear and leave a path for water to reach it.

If a basement bathroom or laundry is planned, see plumbing installation for the drain and pump requirements.

Safety first when water is in the basement

  • Stay out of standing water if the water could reach outlets, the furnace or the electrical panel. Water and live power can be fatal.
  • If you can reach the main breaker without stepping in water, switch it off. If you cannot, call the electric utility or 911 from outside the house.
  • Move to the first floor and keep children and pets away from the water. Floodwater can carry sewage.
  • Photograph the water line and damaged items before you clean up, since your insurer will ask.
  • Do not run a gas appliance that has been in water until a qualified technician has checked it.

Code and permits for waterproofing work

In New Jersey, plumbing work is governed by the Plumbing Subcode of the Uniform Construction Code at N.J.A.C. 5:23-3.15, which adopts the National Standard Plumbing Code. Sump discharge, floor drains and any new plumbing in a basement fall under it.

Whether your job needs a permit depends on the scope, so confirm with the Plainfield Construction Division before work begins.
