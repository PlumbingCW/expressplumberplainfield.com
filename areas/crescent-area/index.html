Crescent Area Historic District, Plainfield.

Plumber in the Crescent Area, Plainfield, NJ

The Crescent Area is a compact district of Victorian houses close to downtown Plainfield. Many were later split into apartments, which changes how a plumbing problem shows up. Call (908) 357-1542 and describe what you see.

What the Crescent Area is

The Crescent Area Historic District is one of ten historic districts the City of Plainfield maps and protects. The city designated it in 1979, and it went on the National Register of Historic Places in December 1980.

The National Park Service nomination draws its edges along Crescent Avenue, First, Second and Third Places, Park Avenue between Crescent and Ninth Street, and parts of Watchung Avenue and Seventh Street. Franklin Place is inside it too.

It is almost entirely residential. The nomination says the line was drawn where land use turns commercial along East Seventh Street. The district is known for Second Empire, Italianate, Queen Anne and Shingle style houses.

Houses built for one family, now often shared

The nomination records that development began in the 1870s and that more than 70 percent of the dwellings went up between 1880 and 1900. Most were built as single-family homes.

It also says many large houses were converted to two and three unit apartments, starting in the 1930s. That history matters for plumbing in a practical way, even though we cannot say how any one house is piped without seeing it.

  • In a converted house, ask which units share a drain line, a water heater or a main shutoff valve.
  • A leak in a ceiling may start in a different unit than the one where the stain appears.
  • Tenants and owners should agree in advance who calls for repairs and who gives access.
  • Keep the location of every shutoff valve written down where all occupants can find it.

What to do before help arrives

  • Shut off the water at the nearest fixture valve or the main valve if water is spraying or pooling.
  • Keep people and pets away from standing water near outlets, appliances or a panel.
  • Move belongings off the floor and photograph the damage for your records.
  • If a drain is backing up, stop running water into it until it is cleared.

Services that come up in older multi-unit and single-family houses

These are general descriptions of the work, not claims about what is common on any Crescent Area street.

Drains and backups

When a tub, sink or toilet drains slowly or backs up, drain cleaning clears the line, and a camera inspection shows whether the pipe itself is the issue.

Hot water

A failing tank shows up as no hot water or a puddle. See water heater repair or replacement.

Leaks

A stain on a ceiling or wall can travel from its source. Leak detection narrows down where the water starts.

Heating pipes

Houses of this age may use hot water or steam heat. Boiler repair covers the boiler and its piping.

Historic district rules to know about

The Historic Preservation Commission requires a Certificate of Appropriateness for changes visible from a public right of way. Most plumbing work happens inside, but a vent, a gas or water line or other exterior work can be visible from the street.

If you are unsure, the Commission offers an informational hearing, and it offers an emergency hearing when urgent repairs are needed to prevent imminent property damage. The city page does not say whether interior work needs review, so ask the Commission at 908-753-3421.

What affects the price of a repair in a house with several units

  • Access.Reaching a fixture may mean arranging entry with tenants and the owner.
  • Shared lines.A repair on a drain or supply line that serves several apartments is a bigger job than one fixture.
  • Scope.Clearing a slow drain costs less than tracing a leak that runs through a ceiling.
  • Parts.Matching an older fixture's connection may need an adapter or a different valve.
  • Records.Owners and tenants may both need a written record for an insurance claim, so ask for one.

Ask for the price and the scope in plain terms before work starts, and call (908) 357-1542 if you want to talk through a repair first.

What we could not confirm

We did not find published figures on pipe materials, drain conditions or plumbing failure rates for this district, so this page makes no such claims. We also did not find a neighborhood association page that sets boundaries beyond the National Register description above.

Crescent Area plumbing questions

Yes. It is a locally designated historic district, added in 1979, and it has been on the National Register of Historic Places since December 1980. The City of Plainfield publishes a map of it.

Start with the owner or property manager, since they control the building. If you are the owner, shut off water to the affected unit if you can, and call us at (908) 357-1542 to describe where the water appears.

The Commission reviews changes visible from a public right of way, which means the outside of a property. Interior repair is usually not visible, but ask the Commission at 908-753-3421 when any part of the work touches the exterior.

Give the street, which fixture or area is affected, whether the water is still running, and whether it is a single house or a multi-unit building. That helps us explain what to expect.
