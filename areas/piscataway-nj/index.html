A township of 60,000 and many sections

Piscataway had 60,804 residents at the 2020 census and covers about 19 square miles in Middlesex County. It was incorporated in 1798, and it includes the ZIP codes 08854 and 08855. Residents describe it by sections, including North Stelton, Possumtown, Randolphville, Society Hill, Fellowship Farm, Raritan Landing and Johnson Park.

A township that size has no single type of home. Older farmhouse neighborhoods, mid-century streets, townhouse communities and apartment complexes all sit within a few miles of each other. We match the plan to the building.

When to call for emergency plumbing in Piscataway

Our dispatch line covers Piscataway 24/7 and we book same-day visits when a technician is free.

  • A water heater leaking in a closet or utility room, especially above another unit.
  • A burst pipe or a water service line failure.
  • A main drain backing up in a multi-unit building.
  • A toilet or sink overflowing with no way to stop it.

Rutgers, apartments and landlord calls

Rutgers University's Busch and Livingston campuses are in the township, and Piscataway Township Schools enrolled 7,331 students in 2024 to 2025. A township with campuses and a large school district has a lot of rental housing and multi-unit buildings.

Landlords and property managers get the same service as homeowners, with one addition. We note which unit, which line and what we found, so the next call has a record. Water heater repair, toilet repair and drain cleaning are the most common visits for these buildings.

Stelton Road, Route 18 and how we reach you

Interstate 287 crosses the township with exits 6 through 9, Route 18 runs along Hoes Lane through the middle for about four miles, and county roads 501 and 529 cross it too. Stelton Road links the township to Plainfield, and we dispatch along it and along Route 28.

Edison borders Piscataway to the east, and we take calls from the Edison side when the job is close to the township line.

Water and the old sections

New Jersey American Water lists Piscataway Township in its Raritan system. Older sections such as New Market, which was known as Quibbletown in the 18th century, and Raritan Landing carry long histories. Homes in those areas are not all old, but some of the plumbing is.

For a house with galvanized supply pipe or a heater past its life, we recommend a planned job over an emergency one. See water heater installation and repiping for the options.

Storms and low ground

Piscataway includes low-lying land along the Raritan River and its brooks. Storms can test basements in those areas, and a working sump pump with a battery backup is a sound precaution. If your basement has taken on water before, see our basement waterproofing page.
