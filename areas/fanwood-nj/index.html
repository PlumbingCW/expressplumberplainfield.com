A small borough with a long history

Fanwood was incorporated on October 2, 1895, from part of what was then Fanwood Township and is now Scotch Plains. At the 2020 census it had 7,774 residents and 2,829 housing units on 1.34 square miles. The ZIP code is 07023.

The Fanwood station is an NJ Transit stop on the Raritan Valley Line, and the borough's layout grew around it. It is smaller and more compact than Scotch Plains next door, with its own character and its own housing.

The Fanwood Park Historic District

The Fanwood Park Historic District is a 52-acre district along North Avenue and North Martine Avenue. It was added to the National Register of Historic Places on May 27, 2004, and it has 57 contributing buildings, including the Fanwood station. The Georgian Revival Urner/Gibbs House dates to 1894.

Homes of that era were built before modern plumbing. Over more than a century the original pipes were extended, patched and replaced in stages. We expect to find a mix of materials, and we tell you what we see before we recommend a fix. A camera inspection is a sensible first step for a repeat clog in a house like this.

Sewer and water in a compact borough

Fanwood is one of the eight municipalities served by the Plainfield Area Regional Sewerage Authority. The borough issues its own sewer bill, and its sewer page says most of that fee is passed along to the authority. It also describes lining sewers during road projects and running a state-required pipe cleaning program.

The borough's page does not say who maintains the lateral from each house, and the answer can differ by address. Call the borough at 908-322-8236 to confirm yours. New Jersey American Water lists Fanwood Borough in its service territory for water.

Seasonal problems in a borough this size

Fanwood's winters test older houses. Pipes along exterior walls and in unheated porches are the first to freeze, and the borough's many older homes have just those spots. A trickle of water on the coldest nights keeps a vulnerable line moving. Opening cabinet doors lets room heat reach pipes under sinks.

In spring and late summer, storms test basements. Robinsons Branch of the Rahway River flows through the borough, and heavy rain saturates the ground around it. Check that downspouts discharge away from the foundation and that any sump pump has power and a free float before the wet season.

Commuters and planned work

Many Fanwood residents commute by rail from the Fanwood station, which makes access for repairs a scheduling question. We plan visits around your hours when the problem is not urgent, and we explain what we need access to before we arrive. For a planned job such as a water heater installation or a bathroom plumbing install, we can set a window that suits your day.
